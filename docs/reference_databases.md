# Reference database provenance

RTBioScan uses separate marker-specific reference databases for animal COI and
plant ITS2. The databases are project-specific derivatives of published
curation efforts; they are not unmodified copies of the upstream datasets.

The default runtime prefixes are:

- COI: `db/COInr98_2024Jun_RioNegro_Brazil`
- ITS2: `db/ITS2nr98_2024Jun_RioNegro_Brazil`

## COI foundation

The COI database was created following the COInr/mkCOInr approach described
by:

Meglécz, E. (2023). COInr and mkCOInr: Building and customizing a
nonredundant barcoding reference database from BOLD and NCBI using a
semi-automated pipeline. *Molecular Ecology Resources*, 23(4), 933-945.
<https://doi.org/10.1111/1755-0998.13756>

COInr integrates COI records from NCBI and BOLD into a coherent taxonomic
system. Its source-level controls include selecting COI records; rejecting
sequences outside 100-2,000 nucleotides or with more than five consecutive
internal `N` bases; excluding unsuitable NCBI environmental, metagenomic,
intron-bearing, or non-COI records; requiring valid Latin taxon names;
assigning coherent taxIDs to BOLD lineages; and removing taxonomically
redundant substrings within taxa.

RTBioScan's COI collection additionally incorporates barcode sequences
generated by the project. The combined records were clustered with CD-HIT at
98% nucleotide identity, and each representative was assigned to the last
common ancestor of the records in its cluster. This project-specific step
reduces redundancy and limits taxonomic sensitivity to the stochastic choice
of a representative sequence.

The separately published canonical COI candidate applies an additional frozen
correctness-first review to the deployed legacy BLAST stream. Its detailed
evidence thresholds, dispositions, and reproducible construction procedure are
recorded in `conf/taxonomy_regression/README.md`.

## ITS2 foundation

The ITS2 database was compiled following the vascular-plant ITS2 curation
approach described by:

Quaresma, A., Ankenbrand, M. J., Garcia, C. A. Y., et al. (2024).
Semi-automated sequence curation for reliable reference datasets in ITS2
vascular plant DNA (meta-)barcoding. *Scientific Data*, 11, 129.
<https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-024-02962-5>

The Quaresma workflow starts from taxonomy-linked GenBank records and defines
five automated reliability filters:

1. Remove fungal sequences identified with VSEARCH SINTAX against the curated
   RDP fungal ITS reference at a 0.90 cutoff.
2. Remove non-ITS2 sequences by comparison with a trusted vascular-plant ITS2
   reference panel, using a 70% identity threshold.
3. Remove records with incomplete taxonomic metadata.
4. Remove Chlorophyta records from the vascular-plant target dataset.
5. For species represented by more than four sequences, remove intraspecific
   outliers through deterministic median-identity thresholds while preventing
   any step from removing 50% or more of the remaining records for that
   species.

The published workflow also supports curator-supplied taxonomic corrections
and the addition of locally generated sequences. It initially restricts source
records to 100-2,000 bases and, for the global dataset described in the paper,
retrieves at most 25 records per species.

RTBioScan extended this approach by integrating the project's NCBI/GenBank and
BOLD-derived ITS2 records with locally generated barcode sequences. As for the
COI reference, the combined collection was clustered with CD-HIT at 98%
nucleotide identity and each representative was assigned to the last common
ancestor of the records in its cluster.

The BOLD integration and 98% CD-HIT/LCA construction are project-specific
extensions. They must not be attributed to the Quaresma workflow itself, which
documents GenBank-based retrieval, automated and manual curation, and custom
sequence addition.

## Release scope

The current public canonical reference bundle contains only the COI FASTA and
its BLAST index. The ITS2 database remains a separate runtime reference and is
not included in, or activated by, the COI release.
